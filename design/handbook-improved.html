Lateral epicondylopathy

Overview

Lateral epicondylopathy ("tennis elbow") is an overuse tendinopathy of the common extensor origin, predominantly involving the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) tendon at the lateral epicondyle. Peak incidence is age 35–54; common in racquet sports and repetitive gripping or wrist-extension work.

MY PEARL

If there is night pain or true neurological symptoms, stop and rethink — this is not tennis elbow until proven otherwise.

Presentation

  • Insidious lateral elbow pain, worse with gripping or lifting with the elbow extended
  • Pain shaking hands, turning a doorknob, or on a backhand stroke
  • No true neurological symptoms — numbness or tingling should prompt reconsidering the diagnosis

Exam findings

Point tenderness ~1 cm distal and anterior to the lateral epicondyle, over the ECRB origin
Pain reproduced with resisted wrist extension (elbow extended, forearm pronated)
Cozen's test and Mill's test
Grip strength with a dynamometer, both sides, at baseline

Working diagnosis

Clinical diagnosis — imaging (ultrasound or MRI) is reserved for atypical presentations, suspected tear, or failure to progress after a full course of conservative management.
